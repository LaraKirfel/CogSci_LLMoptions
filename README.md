# LLM_options
LLM-generated possibilities increase blame attribution


## Abstract

Much of high-level cognition relies on identifying which possibilities are relevant in a situation, and explicit representations of possible actions can predict the extent to which people attribute blame to agents. Building on work showing that large language models (LLMs) sample option spaces differently than humans, we examine how moral evaluation and blame attribution change when alternatives are supplied by an LLM rather than generated by humans. In Study 1, considering LLM-generated options led participants to evaluate the agent’s chosen action more negatively and to attribute more blame than when participants generated options themselves. In Study 2, participants valued LLM-generated options more than human-generated ones and again attributed more blame after considering LLM-generated alternatives. Overall, we find that LLM-generated options occupied a narrower region of the semantic space than human-generated options. We discuss implications for AI’s influence on human cognition and judgment.
